**The most useful user onboarding benchmark is not tour completion. It is whether a new user reaches value and comes back.** The latest large datasets make that distinction clear: activation is strongly associated with retention, while acquisition volume alone is not.

This 2026 edition collects the most defensible public onboarding statistics we could verify, keeps the original scope beside every number, and separates current product benchmarks from older consumer surveys.

## Activation and time-to-value benchmarks

[Userpilot's 2025 SaaS benchmark](https://userpilot.com/saas-product-metrics/) reports a **37.5% average activation rate** from 62 B2B companies. It reports an average **time to value of one day and 12 hours** from the same group.

Those values need context. Activation is a product-specific event: sending a first campaign, inviting a teammate, exporting a file, or completing another action tied to value. A vague “signed in” event will inflate your rate without proving that onboarding worked.

[Amplitude's analysis of more than 10,600 products](https://amplitude.com/explore/analytics/product-analytics-guide) gives a complementary behavioral threshold: a **7% day-seven return rate** places a product in the top 25% for activation. More importantly, **69% of top performers in seven-day activation were also top performers in three-month retention**.

The practical reading is not “make onboarding longer.” It is “identify the earliest meaningful value event, then remove the delay before it.”

## Checklist and feature-adoption benchmarks

Userpilot reports:

- **19.2% average onboarding-checklist completion** across 188 companies, with a **10.1% median**.
- **24.5% average core-feature adoption** across 181 companies, with a **16.5% median**.

The difference between mean and median matters. A handful of strong programs can lift the average, so the median is usually the more realistic comparison for a typical SaaS product.

A checklist is also an implementation, not an outcome. If users complete steps that do not lead to value, the checklist can improve while retention stays flat. Track checklist completion alongside activation and time-to-value.

## Retention benchmarks

Userpilot reports **46.9% average month-one retention** across 83 B2B SaaS companies and a **45.25% median**.

Amplitude's B2B cut shows how wide the performance gap becomes later: [top B2B products retained 15.6% of users at three months, versus 2.5% for the median](https://amplitude.com/blog/b2b-technology-product-benchmarks). That is more than a sixfold difference. The underlying cross-industry report used anonymized data from more than 2,600 companies, spanning September 2023 through September 2024.

Amplitude also found no meaningful relationship between being in the top quartile for acquisition and being in the top quartile for retention. More signups do not repair a leaky first experience.

## What users say about onboarding content

The most frequently repeated onboarding-preference numbers come from [Wyzowl's survey of 216 people in early 2020](https://wyzowl.com/customer-onboarding-statistics-2020-report/). They are older consumer perceptions—not present-day SaaS telemetry—but remain useful when properly labeled:

- **63%** considered onboarding content when deciding whether to buy.
- **65%** preferred video for learning a new product or service.
- **86%** said they would be more likely to remain loyal when a business invests in welcoming and educational onboarding content.

Wyzowl republishes those figures with context in its [onboarding video case-study collection](https://wyzowl.com/customer-onboarding-video-success-stories/). They support the importance of understandable education, but they do not prove that video—or any single format—causes retention.

## How to use these numbers without fooling yourself

1. **Define activation before measuring it.** Choose an action that represents value, not merely activity.
2. **Segment the benchmark.** Product complexity, price, audience, and sales motion can make a cross-industry average a poor target.
3. **Pair leading and lagging metrics.** Track time-to-value and activation together with month-one and three-month retention.
4. **Use medians when available.** They are less distorted by unusually strong programs.
5. **Test the guidance model.** A scripted tour, checklist, video, searchable help, or [on-demand guide](/learn/onboarding-without-building-tours/) should be judged by the value event it helps users reach.
